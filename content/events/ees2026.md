## Our sessions

Both sessions are on Wednesday 28 October.

<ol class="ukes-programme-list">
<li>
<p><strong>Interrogating evaluation: a live experiment and conversation, with a little help from AI</strong>: Innovative Modality · Session S1d, Strand S01 New Methods · 3.7 The Studio · 13:00-14:30 · Silva Ferretti (Independent Consultant), Steve Powell (Causal Map Ltd)</p>
<p>Participants are interviewed live by <a href="https://qualiainterviews.com">Qualia</a>, an AI interviewer, about where evaluation is going, what new capacities matter most, and what risks evaluators should now take seriously. Qualia then synthesises the interviews. The session treats that synthesis as a starting point: small groups discuss the same questions, some with AI support and some without, and in plenary we compare what each way of working made easier, harder, more visible or less visible.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Non-mosquitoes do not cause non-mosquito bites</strong>: Individual paper · Session S3e "From Stories to Data", Strand S03 Responsiveness · 3.1 C · 16:00, in the session running 15:00-16:30 · Steve Powell (Causal Map Ltd), Fiona Remnant (Bath SDR / QuIP)</p>
<p>People mostly do not speak in variables, yet most ways of coding "what causes what" assume they do. We argue that when you are modelling what people believe, the default should be to code causal claims as the speaker made them: mosquitoes cause mosquito bites, with opposite labels kept separate and combined only when the analysis needs it. This keeps the stakeholder's logic rather than the analyst's, and it is also the easiest coding for people and for AI. The full conventions are in <a href="https://garden.causalmap.app/minimalist/">Minimalist coding for causal mapping</a> and <a href="https://garden.causalmap.app/combining-opposites/">Combining opposites</a>.</p>
</li>
</ol>
